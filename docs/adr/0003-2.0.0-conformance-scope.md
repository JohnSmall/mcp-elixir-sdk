# ADR-003: 2.0.0 conformance scope — core only, authorization profile excluded, client held to the same bar

**Status: ACCEPTED** — PO, 2026-08-02, **accepted in full without changes**. (PM draft 2026-08-02.)
**Supersedes:** nothing. **Refines:** ADR-002 sub-decisions 3 and 4.
**Canonical location:** `docs/adr/0003-2.0.0-conformance-scope.md` — repo is source of truth; this page is the mirror. _(Landing pending — see Consequences.)_

---

## Context

ADR-002 committed the project to **full conformance to the 2026-07-28 revision in its entirety, not a subset**, delivered as a breaking 2.0.0. That commitment was made against the locked RC, before the spec-first pass existed. Three things have since sharpened what it means in practice:

1. **The spec published final on 2026-07-28**, with no change required to work already built against the locked RC (PO ruling 1 vindicated).
2. **The gap register ([D1 v5](https://vidhya-trading.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ElixirMCPS/pages/239861761))** pinned the core/extension boundary precisely, and [D5](https://vidhya-trading.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ElixirMCPS/pages/239927297) established that the revision's authorization hardening binds the **client and the Authorization Server**, not the server — server-side authorization obligations come from _earlier_ Final SEPs, not this revision.
3. **Sprint 3 shipped the stateless core** (`main` @ `2c9a71a`): stateless protocol, stateless Streamable-HTTP transport, identity seam v2.

"In its entirety" therefore needs a stated denominator, because three different denominators are defensible — the core specification, the core plus extension track, and the core plus the authorization profile — and a 2.0.0 that claims conformance without naming which one would be making an unevidenced claim. The project has spent a sprint learning not to do that (Working Procedure Overrides **A2**, **A4**).

## Decision

**2.0.0 targets the complete core specification of MCP 2026-07-28, on both server and client sides, excluding the authorization profile and the extension track. The 2.0.0 conformance claim states that exclusion explicitly.**

Resolved sub-decisions (PO, 2026-08-02):

1. **Core specification: in, in full.** Every core requirement in the D1 v5 gap register, including the items outstanding after Sprint 3 — `subscriptions/listen` (D2's ADD half), the extensions _negotiation surface_ (SEP-2133, J1), and JSON Schema 2020-12 (SEP-2106, K1).
2. **Extension track: deferred.** Tasks (SEP-2663) and MCP Apps are out of 2.0.0 and reconsidered afterwards. The negotiation surface is core and in; extension _content_ is out. This confirms ADR-002 sub-decision 3 as read by PO ruling 3 (2026-07-23), reaffirmed after the core shipped.
3. **Authorization profile: out of 2.0.0.** Client OAuth 2.1 (CAND-A) and the revision's client-side auth-hardening SEPs (SEP-2468, SEP-837, SEP-2352, CIMD-over-DCR) are out of scope. The SDK's security posture is unchanged and unaffected: caller identity is established by the **host's authenticated pipeline** and threaded per request by the identity seam — the invariant ADR-002 and the D2 design spec exist to protect. Authorization is _how the host authenticates_; the SDK's job begins after that.
4. **Client-side conformance: held to the same bar as the server** — 100% of the core specification, excluding the authorization profile. This ends the previous position, under which client-side coverage was a per-ticket charter question.
5. **No support for 2025-11-25, on either side.** Package-level cutover stands (ADR-002 sub-decision 5). Old-shape requests fail fast with `UnsupportedProtocolVersion` (-32022). Neither the server nor the client negotiates down. **Consequence for measurement:** conformance-harness scenarios written against 2025-11-25 are **expected failures by design, not defects** — the conformance claim is measured against the final revision's scenarios only, and any old-revision scenarios must be enumerated and excluded with that reason recorded.
6. **The conformance claim is worded to its scope.** README, CHANGELOG, HexDocs and the release notes say, in substance: _full conformance to the MCP 2026-07-28 core specification, server and client; the authorization profile and extension-track features (Tasks, MCP Apps) are not implemented in this release._ No unqualified "fully conformant" claim ships.

## Why

**Naming the denominator is the point.** "Full conformance" with three defensible readings is not a claim a consumer can rely on. Stating the exclusions makes the claim falsifiable — the standard this project already applies to its own reports (Overrides A2/A4).

**Authorization is separable, and separating it is honest.** D5 showed the revision adds no new server-side authorization obligations; the auth-hardening SEPs bind the client and the Authorization Server. Excluding them removes a substantial, independently-scoped body of work from the release path without touching the identity invariant, which lives at a different layer.

**Holding the client to the server's bar closes a real gap.** The client-mode conformance baseline was **1/42** against the _old_ revision — the client has been the weaker half throughout. A stateless-core SDK whose client cannot demonstrate conformance is half a migration. The cost is honest sizing work, scheduled as an early measurement (roadmap W4a) rather than discovered late.

**One deferral is cheap; a wrong claim is not.** Extensions can ship in 2.1; a conformance claim, once published to Hex, is what consumers build on.

## Consequences

* **Sprint 4** ("conformance complete") carries `subscriptions/listen`, the extensions negotiation surface, JSON Schema 2020-12, and the conformance measurement — **client work sized against W4a's early measurement**, not assumed.
* **Sprint 5** ("ship 2.0.0") carries docs/deprecation conformance, remaining client acceptance, EMFA cutover, and the PO-gated release.
* **The release ticket owns the claim wording** in README, CHANGELOG, HexDocs and release notes, verified from the **packaged** contents (Overrides A3).
* `~> 1.1` stays frozen except security fixes (ADR-002 sub-decision 2). No backport of stateless-core work.
* **EMFA** cuts over to 2.0.0 and remains the external acceptance signal (ADR-002 sub-decision 3). Per the standing PO directive, EMFA's convenience carries no weight in SDK design decisions.
* **Post-2.0.0 backlog:** extension track (Tasks, MCP Apps), the authorization profile / CAND-A, and the hardening items carried from Sprint 3.
* **If the conformance harness still lacks stateless-core scenarios** (it had none at the Sprint 3 measurement), the claim rests on our own ported acceptance evidence, and **says so** rather than implying harness certification.
* **Repo landing (open action):** CC lands `docs/adr/0003-2.0.0-conformance-scope.md` and updates `docs/adr/README.md`. Docs-only — no version bump, no CHANGELOG entry. Per Overrides **D1**, commit subject follows Global rules #5/#20 (`[{TICKET_KEY}] <title>`). _Timing is a PO call: land now under the ADR-001/002 precedent (both landed 2026-07-22, before Sprint 3 opened), or as the first act of Sprint 4 under a ticket. Landing before planning is preferable — every Sprint 4 ticket brief cites this ADR as its scope contract._

## Alternatives considered

* **Include the authorization profile in 2.0.0.** Rejected: substantial client-side work, independently scoped, on the critical path to a release that is otherwise ready; it does not affect the identity invariant.
* **Ship the extension track (Tasks) in 2.0.0.** Rejected: extension-track SEPs version independently of the core; deferring costs a minor release, not a breaking one.
* **Leave client-side conformance as a per-ticket charter question.** Rejected: it produced deferrals in two consecutive tickets (MES-9's client charter, MES-10 §3.3) with no owner for the total. A single bar with an early measurement is more honest and more plannable.
* **Claim unqualified "full conformance."** Rejected as an unevidenced claim — precisely the failure mode Overrides A2/A4 exist to prevent.
